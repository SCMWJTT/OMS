# Acme Caribbean Energy (ACE) — Company Profile

*Fictional reference company created by Cynmark Systems Limited for demonstration projects (OMS document publishing, supply chain, and others). All people, assets and lease names are invented. Profile as at 21 November 2026.*

---

## 1. Overview

Acme Caribbean Energy Limited (ACE) is a mid-size Trinidad and Tobago crude oil and natural gas producer with about **300 staff**. It was formed in 2004 and has grown by taking on mature land leases and small offshore fields that larger operators no longer prioritised.

ACE does three things:

- **Produces** crude oil and gas from small offshore fields in the **Gulf of Paria** and from **land leases** in Central and South Trinidad.
- **Processes** all of its production at its own **Central Processing Facility (CPF)** in Central Trinidad.
- **Sells** gas to **NGC** (The National Gas Company of Trinidad and Tobago) under a gas sales agreement, and sells crude to buyers, shipped by tanker or barge from the CPF jetty.

ACE is the operator on its licences and has joint venture partners on some of them.

### How product moves

```
Reservoirs → Wells (land & offshore) → Pommerac A / well pads & gathering stations
          → Gulf of Paria pipelines / land flowlines → Central Processing Facility
          → Sales gas to NGC  |  Crude stored and shipped by tanker or barge
```

### How materials move

```
Suppliers → Point Lisas Shore Base (warehouse) → Supply boats → Gulf of Paria platforms
                                              → Road transport → Land leases and CPF
```

## 4. The cross-functional structure

An oil and gas company cannot be run through its reporting lines alone. ACE's chart has **two kinds of line**.

### Solid lines: who you work for

Every employee has one line manager in one function. Solid lines decide day-to-day priorities, performance reviews and budgets.

### Dotted lines: who sets your technical standard

ACE has **32 discipline leads**. Each one:

- lives in the function where the work happens (a solid line), and
- holds a **dotted line to the VP Engineering & Projects** through the **Technical Leadership Council (TLC)**, which the VP chairs. Elena Farfan (Manager, Technical Excellence) acts as secretary.

Each discipline lead is the **Technical Authority** for the discipline. They own its standards, endorse changes (MOCs), approve technical deviations and assure the quality of the work, whichever function does it.

| Home function | Discipline leads |
|---|---|
| Engineering & Projects (12) | Process, Static Mechanical, Piping, Pipelines, Structural & Civil, Electrical, Instrumentation & Control, Fire & Gas and Safety Systems, Materials & Corrosion, Subsea, Metering & Measurement, Cost & Planning |
| Exploration & Development (5) | Geology, Geophysics, Petrophysics, Reservoir Engineering, Production Technology |
| HSE & Compliance (4) | Process Safety, Environmental, Occupational Health & Hygiene, Human Factors |
| Operations (4) | Rotating Equipment, Inspection, Reliability & Maintenance, Production Chemistry |
| Wells (3) | Drilling Engineering, Completions, Well Integrity |
| IT, Data & Knowledge (2) | Information Management, Telecoms & OT Cyber Security |
| Supply Chain Management (2) | Marine, Quality |

This is why **Engineering & Projects sits at the centre** of the ACE map. It is a mid-size function (34 people), but every technical line in the company runs through it.

### Area Authority and Technical Authority

Two kinds of authority cross the organisation and appear throughout ACE's procedures:

- **Area Authority:** the person responsible for everything that happens at a location. That's the OIMs for Pommerac A, the Offshore Operations Manager for the unmanned platforms and pipelines, the Land Operations Manager for the leases, and the Plant Manager for the CPF. They issue permits and release work.
- **Technical Authority:** the discipline lead who decides whether something is technically acceptable. They endorse designs, changes and deviations.

Many of ACE's procedures depend on these two agreeing. MOC, permit to work and deviation control are examples.

### The document problem ACE is trying to solve

ACE's management system is very document-heavy, especially for **asset integrity and engineering excellence**: engineering standards, written schemes of examination, performance standards, procedures and work instructions.

In most companies the link between those documents and the OMS is kept **outside** the documents, in a conformance matrix or a separately built OMS web portal. It falls out of date as soon as documents are revised. Over time staff stop trusting the portal and go back to searching for PDFs, and the OMS context is lost. Many companies end up with documents in SharePoint folders and no OMS view at all.

ACE's approach, under its **Operational Document Publishing Standard**, is to keep the OMS context **inside the approved Word documents**:

1. **OMS tags** under the title and each heading say which IOGP 510 expectations that section addresses.
2. **Semantic paragraph styles** mark requirements, responsibilities, risk controls, warnings, approval gates and record requirements.
3. **Document metadata** records the owner, function, process and role register.

Authors simply produce a properly formatted document. A reader program then builds the web views automatically:

- an OMS wheel by element
- a network map of documents
- coverage and gap views
- views by role and by function

An **auditor** checks every document at the gateway: missing tags, invalid tag numbers, "shall" statements not styled as requirements, missing metadata.

The Information Management Lead's gatekeeper role remains, because someone must own the nomenclature. Automated checks, and later AI-suggested tagging, reduce that role from hours of manual work per document to a review.

## 6. Using ACE in other projects

ACE is designed to be reused. Some useful anchors for other projects:

- **Supply chain:**
  - Point Lisas Shore Base, supply boats and crew boats to the Gulf of Paria, road logistics to the leases and the CPF.
  - Rig, workover and well services contracts.
  - Contractor HSE pre-qualification and local content commitments.
  - An 18-person SCM function with Procurement, Contracts, and Logistics & Materials teams, plus Marine and Quality discipline leads.
- **Commercial:** a gas sales agreement with NGC, crude sales and liftings, joint venture partners and partner billing.
- **Asset integrity:** platforms, pipelines, land flowlines, tank farm, rotating equipment, safety-critical elements, inspection and corrosion programmes.
- **People and competence:** 300 named staff with grades, degrees, hire dates and job descriptions; offshore rotation and shift patterns; a recognised union.
